# TaskFlow 1.14
# User's Guide

A practical guide to tasks, calendars, notes, shopping, and Watch Later.
Prepared October 7, 2026. This guide describes the TaskFlow 1.14 feature set, including the latest interface improvements. Update your installed app if a described control is missing. Layout and available controls can vary with screen size, permissions, list type, and account capabilities.

## 01 | Start here

TaskFlow brings your Apple Reminders, calendar events, and TaskFlow notes into one workspace. Use it for a quick shopping trip or a more structured day plan; you do not need to configure every feature before starting.

### Your first five minutes
1. Open TaskFlow and choose **Set Up TaskFlow** on the welcome screen. You can return to it from Settings.
2. Allow Reminders access to connect your lists and tasks. The guided flow also offers calendar access; Calendar is optional.
3. Review suggested layouts for lists whose names resemble Shopping, Groceries, Read Later, or Watch Later. Tap a suggestion to apply that layout.
4. Open a list and add one item. A title is enough to begin; dates and detailed fields can come later.
5. Open Today to see due tasks, overdue work, priorities, and calendar events.

### Choose a starting point
- **Everyday tasks:** open Tasks, choose a list, and use New Task.
- **Shopping:** choose a Shopping & Groceries list and use Add Item.
- **Movies and series:** choose a Reading & Watch Later list and use Save Link.
- **Notes:** open Notes and create a note. Browser access is covered in chapters 10 and 11.

### Where your information lives
Tasks belong to Apple Reminders lists, and events belong to calendar accounts. TaskFlow notes and supported app information use TaskFlow's iCloud syncing. Your TaskFlow notes do not appear in Apple's Notes app. A browser login uses your Apple Account; there is no separate TaskFlow notes account to create.

**Good first habit:** begin with one list and a few real items. Add specialist fields only when they help you decide or act.

## 02 | Guided setup and permissions

Open **Settings > Set Up TaskFlow** whenever you want to reconnect the main features or review layout suggestions. The guided flow connects Reminders, requests calendar access when needed, and selects available event calendars. It does not replace your existing list items.

### Reminders
Allow access to display, create, edit, complete, and delete reminders. If you decline access, connected reminder lists cannot be loaded. Use the permissions controls in TaskFlow; when access has already been denied, follow **Open System Settings** or **Manage in Settings** to change it in iOS Settings.

### Calendar
Calendar access lets TaskFlow show events and find open time. **Use my calendars** selects your available event calendars. You can refine the choice under Settings > Calendars > Customize. Read-only calendars may be visible even though you cannot create events in them.

### Notifications
Notifications are optional. Enable them when you want task alerts, bill deadlines, routine timer alerts, or episode reminders. A saved date alone is not proof that an alert will appear: review the alert settings and notification permission.

### Other permissions
Photos, files, microphone, speech recognition, and location-related controls appear when relevant to a feature. For example, dictation needs audio permissions; an attachment may need photo or file access. Grant only the access needed for the feature you are using.

### Sensible defaults
In **Settings > Tasks & Lists**, choose a Default List for tasks created outside a specific list. In Calendars, choose a writable Default Calendar for new events. If you leave these on their automatic choices, check the destination before saving.

**Tip:** setup is revisitable. A permission you skip today does not prevent you from using unrelated features.

## 03 | Find your way around

TaskFlow's main areas are **Today, Tasks, Calendar, Notes, and Search**. On a wider screen, navigation may appear in a sidebar rather than the same compact arrangement used on iPhone.

### Today
Use Today to decide what needs attention now. It combines task counts, available time, Focus Next, Top 3 Today, and calendar information. Customize the sections to keep this screen useful rather than crowded.

### Tasks
Browse your reminder lists, pinned destinations, and smart lists. Open a list to see its items. A list's layout depends on its chosen type: a grocery list emphasizes quantities and stores, while a media list emphasizes links and progress.

### Calendar
View tasks alongside calendar events, inspect scheduling conflicts, and find open time. Task due dates and event times are related planning information, but remain different kinds of records.

### Notes
Create independent notes or link a note to a task or event. Use folders, tags, pinning, and search to find information again.

### Search and menus
Use Search for items across your workspace. Search fields in individual views may narrow the content of that view. Some task searches support typing a tag with **#**. Clear search and filters when something seems missing.

Look for the **plus** button to add information, **List Tools** in specialized lists, and the **More** menu for additional task actions. Touch and hold an item where supported to reveal contextual actions. Open the item itself for the full detail screen.

**Tip:** pin the lists you use most often. You can manage them from **Manage Pinned Lists** or **Pinned Lists** without changing their contents.

## 04 | Lists and useful layouts

A reminder list is the destination that owns a task. A TaskFlow layout changes how that list is presented and which extra fields are offered.

### Create or customize a list
1. Open Tasks and choose **New List**.
2. Give the list a clear name and create it.
3. Use **Settings > Tasks & Lists > Edit Reminder Lists** to edit an existing list.
4. Choose **List Type**, color, and icon; save your changes.

Common names can produce a one-tap suggestion: Shopping or Groceries suggests Shopping & Groceries; Read Later and Watch Later suggest Reading & Watch Later. Suggestions are offered for standard lists, rather than automatically changing an already customized list.

### Available list types
- **Standard:** everyday reminders.
- **Shopping & Groceries:** quantity, unit, category, store, and estimated price.
- **Projects & Work:** sections, milestones, outcomes, blockers, and next actions.
- **Household & Chores:** rooms, instructions, and seasonal information.
- **Packing & Travel:** quantities, categories, essentials, and packing stages.
- **Bills & Renewals:** amounts, payment status, and renewal deadlines.
- **Reading & Watch Later:** saved links, media details, and progress.
- **Errands:** destinations, opening hours, and preparation.
- **Appointments & Follow-ups:** contacts, questions, preparation, and outcomes.
- **Routines & Checklists:** ordered steps, instructions, and timers.

### Templates
Some types offer **Include Starter Template**. This saves a template under List Tools > Templates; start it when you are ready. Existing reminders remain in place. Custom templates are useful for repeated trips, chores, and checklists.

**Remember:** changing a layout does not move tasks to a different Reminders account or turn reminders into calendar events.

## 05 | Create, edit, and complete tasks

Use **New Task** or the plus button to open the task editor. When you create a task from a particular list, review that list as the destination. Tasks created elsewhere use your default or automatic destination.

### Start with the basics
Enter a clear title, such as "Book dentist appointment." Add notes or a web link if they will help you act. A due date and time are optional. Choose the list, priority, flag, or status when relevant, then save.

### More detail when needed
Open **Details** for repeat settings, alerts, start date, location, tags, and attachments. The editor also offers subtasks, dependencies, and comments. Specialized lists provide their own fields, such as grocery quantity or a bill's renewal date.

### Completion and status
Mark a task complete with its completion control. Completed tasks can be shown in a completed section or through display settings. Reopen a completed task if you checked it off by mistake. Status labels such as Active, Waiting, and Blocked describe work in progress; completion is a separate action.

### Dates versus alerts
A due date says when work is expected. An alert determines when you are reminded. In task Details, check the actual alert configuration rather than assuming a date will notify you. Additional alerts can be relative to a due time or set to a specific time.

### Repeating work
Choose a repeat pattern for recurring tasks. Before deleting recurring work, check what you want to retain in Apple Reminders. TaskFlow also offers reusable list templates when you want to create a fresh checklist rather than repeat one task.

**Example:** create "Take recycling out," give it the next collection date, add a reminder at a useful time, and choose a weekly repeat if that matches your schedule.

## 06 | Capture quickly, then review

**Quick Capture** is a faster way to turn a short phrase into a task or event. Open it from an available plus or task menu.

### Capture a task
1. Choose the task capture mode and enter a phrase describing the work.
2. Review the parsed title in Preview.
3. Choose the destination list.
4. Check whether a due date was recognized. Correct it, add a time, or turn the due-date option off.
5. Review the reminder choice and choose **Continue** to proceed through the capture flow.

### Capture an event
Choose Event, then review the title, writable calendar, start time, and end time. Check both date and duration before committing the event. An event is saved to the selected calendar, rather than to a reminder list.

### Important review habit
Date recognition is a convenience, not a substitute for checking. If the preview says **No date recognized**, set one yourself when needed. If it recognizes an unintended date, change or remove it before saving.

### Capture a link from another app
Use the system Share menu for supported content and choose TaskFlow. Shared videos can route to Watch Later and articles to Read Later. In **Settings > Share Extension**, choose the destination lists; Automatic is available. Shared links may remain queued until TaskFlow opens and imports them.

### Batch shopping capture
For a grocery list, use **Paste Items** or **Filters & Shopping Tools > Paste Several Items**. Review the proposed items, quantities, store, and duplicates before importing. This is faster than opening a separate editor for each line.

**Tip:** capture now, organize afterward. A useful title and correct destination are more important than filling every optional field.

## 07 | Today, Focus Next, and priorities

Today is your daily starting point. The overview counts tasks due today, overdue tasks, and events. The available-time area uses connected calendar information and your planning preferences.

### Focus Next: simple task triage
Focus Next presents one suggested task with two main choices:
- **Mark Complete:** finish the task and let the view move on.
- **Choose Another:** cycle to another candidate without completing the current task.

Tap the task title to open its details. Choosing another does not change the task's due date or delete it. Suggestions consider actionable work; do not treat them as an obligation to follow a fixed order.

### Top 3 Today
Choose **Choose up to three priorities**, or **Manage** beside Top 3 Today. Select the work you most want to finish. These priorities do not replace the tasks' due dates. Use them to make important work visible even when it is not scheduled for today.

### Overdue work
Open the overdue section to review unfinished tasks. Use **Plan Overdue Tasks** or an available reschedule action when you want to assign a realistic new time. Completing, rescheduling, and removing a date are different decisions; choose the one that reflects the work.

### Customize Today
Open **Customize Today** or the section-selection control. Show, hide, and reorder available sections. Use **Restore Default Layout** to return to the standard arrangement.

### A practical daily routine
Check events first, select your Top 3, and use Focus Next for quick triage. When a task needs more time than you have, open its details or planning tools rather than marking it complete simply to dismiss it.

## 08 | Calendars and available time

Open Calendar to see events and dated tasks together. Use **Today** to return to the current date, **Go to Date** to jump elsewhere, and the available view controls to change the calendar layout.

### Select calendars
In **Settings > Calendars**, choose **Use my calendars** to select available event calendars. Expand **Customize** for individual selections. Choose a writable Default Calendar for new events. A read-only subscribed calendar can be displayed, but its events cannot necessarily be edited.

### Decide what blocks your time
The **Affects Availability** settings determine which calendars count toward open-time suggestions and conflict checks. Reference calendars, holidays, or other calendars that do not represent your commitments can remain visible while being excluded from availability calculations. This choice does not change an event's Show As value.

### Create and edit events
Use **New Event**, review the calendar, title, start and end, all-day setting, and any available details. Open an existing event for Event Details and Edit. For recurring events, pay attention to whether an operation affects one occurrence or future occurrences.

### Calendar preferences
Open **Calendar Settings** for working hours, preferred focus times, and layout preferences. Working hours help the planner offer useful slots; they do not move your existing events automatically.

### Filters
Calendar filters can restrict lists, search content, or a date range. If an expected item is absent, review the selected calendars and filters. Use Clear Filters before assuming the item was deleted.

**Example:** keep a holiday calendar visible but turn off its Affects Availability option. Your appointments still block time, while a holiday reference entry does not consume the entire day in planning suggestions.

## 09 | Plan work and review conflicts

TaskFlow's planning tools help you find time for work and review overlaps. Suggested schedules require your review; they are not a guarantee that every real-world commitment is represented.

### Find time for one task
Open **Plan Time** or an available **Find Time** action. Select the task and review its estimated duration, date range, and available-time preferences. Choose **Find Open Slots**, then inspect the suggested time before applying it.

### Build a day plan
Use **Build a Day Plan** or **Plan My Day**, choose the work to include, and select **Suggest a Day Plan**. Review the proposed schedule before saving. Check ordering, duration, and conflicts rather than accepting a plan solely because a slot is open.

### Conflict Checker
Open **Conflict Checker**, select a relevant date range, and refresh the results when needed. Possible resolutions include editing a time, finding another time, or marking an event Free when that accurately describes it. Some operations depend on whether the event's calendar is writable.

### Dependencies and blockers
A task with unfinished dependencies may not be ready to work on. Add or remove dependencies in the task's detail controls. In project lists, record a Blocked Reason and identify Next Actions to make the next practical step clear.

### Keep dates deliberate
Choosing Top 3 priorities does not assign due dates. Calendar selection does not move events. A planning preview does not mean a change has already been saved. Read the confirmation or preview for each operation.

**Good practice:** use realistic durations and leave breathing room between commitments. Calendar data can omit travel, preparation, or commitments stored in an account you have not connected.

## 10 | Notes in TaskFlow

Use Notes for information that should stay available without turning every sentence into a reminder. Notes can stand alone or be linked to a task or event.

### Create and organize
1. Open Notes and choose **New Note**.
2. Add a title and content.
3. Choose a folder or **New Folder** when you want to organize it.
4. Add tags and, if useful, link it to a task or event.
5. Save using the editor's save control.

Folders and tags serve different purposes. A folder gives a note a home; tags help group related notes across folders. Pin notes you want to find quickly. Search and note filters can help find linked notes or unfinished checklists.

### Rich content
Use available formatting and checklist controls to structure text. Native notes also support drawings and attachments. Photo and file attachments may require permission or a file selection. A drawing is edited in TaskFlow, rather than in the browser.

### Version history and recovery
Open a note's menu and choose **Version History** to inspect saved versions. Use **Restore This Version** only after reviewing the version you want. The editor may retain a recovery draft after an interrupted edit; recover useful text before discarding that draft.

### Syncing
TaskFlow note syncing uses the app's iCloud flow. **Settings > iCloud Sync > Sync Now** is useful when checking another device or preparing browser access. Allow syncing to finish before comparing versions.

**Important distinction:** TaskFlow Notes is not Apple's Notes app. Connecting iCloud does not make TaskFlow notes appear in Apple Notes, and private notes are not automatically shared with people who share a Reminders list.

## 11 | Web Notes: start and work safely

Web Notes lets you read and edit TaskFlow note text in a browser at **https://modcaststudios.app/notes**. You can also use **Open Notes in Browser** from the Notes menu when available.

### First-time access
1. Open the current TaskFlow app on your iPhone or iPad while signed into your intended Apple Account.
2. Run **Settings > iCloud Sync > Sync Now** and allow the sync to complete.
3. Open the notes website and choose **Continue with iCloud**.
4. Authenticate with the same Apple Account used by TaskFlow.
5. Confirm that your notes appear before editing.

If the browser says the account needs preparation, return to TaskFlow and sync. The native app needs to prepare note access first. Ordinary users do not need a CloudKit API token, a Cloudflare account, or developer-console setup.

### What you can do
Create and edit text notes; use headings, bold, italic, lists, checklists, quotes, and links; organize with folders, tags, pinning, and search. Use the save status, explicit save controls, and refresh controls to confirm work has reached iCloud.

### Native-only content
Browser editing focuses on note text. Existing native drawings and attachment information are retained by the sync flow, but drawing tools and browser attachment uploads are not part of this workflow. Return to TaskFlow to manage that content.

### Sample notes
**Explore with sample notes** is a demonstration. Sample mode is not your authenticated iCloud library and should not be used as proof that your own notes are syncing.

### Before leaving
Wait for a successful save indication. On a shared computer, sign out when finished. Resolve or download an unsaved draft before agreeing to discard it.

## 12 | Web Notes conflicts, drafts, and Trash

A conflict appears when your browser draft and the saved note have both changed. This can happen when you edit the same note on your phone and in the browser, or keep multiple editing tabs open.

### "This note changed elsewhere"
Compare **Your draft** with **Other version**, then choose:
- **Keep Both:** retain the versions as separate notes so you can compare and combine them later.
- **Keep Mine:** use your browser draft as the version to retain.
- **Keep Other Version:** use the other saved version instead of your current draft.
- **Decide Later:** postpone the decision while preserving the current draft for that editing session.

When unsure, Keep Both is the easiest way to avoid losing a difference you have not reviewed. A conflict is not automatically evidence that syncing is broken.

### Failed or interrupted saves
Read the save message before closing the tab. Use **Download Draft** when offered to keep a Markdown copy of unsaved text. Reload recovery may restore a session draft, but a session draft is not a replacement for a confirmed iCloud save. Clearing browser data or ending a session can remove local recovery information.

### Trash and Restore
Use the browser's Trash view to find trashed notes and restore them when needed. Treat Trash controls separately from native delete actions; the two interfaces do not have identical recovery tools. Do not assume that every permanent-delete action can be undone later.

### Recovered notes
Concurrent native and browser edits may be preserved as separate recovered notes. Open both versions, compare content, and retain the information you need before cleaning up extras.

**Prevention:** finish and save an edit on one device before switching to another. Refresh the browser after a native sync when you want to verify the latest version.

## 13 | Shopping: build a useful list

Choose the **Shopping & Groceries** layout for your shopping list. The list emphasizes item names and quantities, with categories, stores, and prices when useful.

### Add an item
1. Open the shopping list and choose **Add Item**.
2. Enter the item name and review its quantity and unit.
3. Choose a category and store if relevant.
4. Add an optional estimated price or substitute.
5. Save the item.

A default quantity is useful for a single purchase. Use a unit such as package or bottle when it clarifies what the quantity means. You can edit individual items later without changing the entire list.

### Set a default store
Open **List Tools > Customize List** or the list's settings and use **Default Store**. Choose an existing store or **Add Store**. New items use the default, while existing items retain their stores. A selected store filter takes precedence for new captures; always review the destination store in the editor.

### Per-item stores
Change Store on an individual item when that purchase belongs somewhere else. For example, keep your default grocery store while assigning hardware supplies to a hardware store.

### Store chips and filters
Tap **All Stores**, a named store, or **No Store** to change the visible items. Favorites and other shopping filters are available in Filters & Shopping Tools. Active filters are shown near the top; **Clear Filters** removes the store and favorites restrictions.

### Categories and aisle order
Use **Categories & Aisle Order** to arrange shopping categories. Categories can group purchases in a practical walking order. When grouping by store, category information can remain useful within each store's items.

**Tip:** use consistent store names. Store choices help you reuse existing spelling instead of creating near-duplicates.

## 14 | Shopping: buy, estimate, and repeat

Shopping rows have larger purchase controls for easier use with one thumb. The main line emphasizes the name and quantity; secondary information is reduced when the section or store filter already provides context.

### During a shopping trip
Tap the completion circle when an item is purchased. Purchased items move out of the active list into the purchased area. Reopen the item or use available Undo controls if you marked it by mistake. The finished state reads **All done shopping** when the list's open work is complete.

### Sticky estimates
The bottom summary remains visible while scrolling. It shows estimated total, remaining cost, missing-price information, and budget information when configured. The total reflects the list scope and store filter; it is not necessarily the total of every item in every store.

### Prices and budget
Tap an item's price control to enter an estimate. Type the number normally, including a decimal separator when you want fractional currency. Price entry no longer inserts decimal places automatically while typing. The estimate is remembered for the matching item, store, and unit.

Set an optional budget in **Customize List > Shopping Budget**. Estimates use your current currency and exclude tax. Missing prices can make the estimate lower than the amount you ultimately pay. **Estimate Missing Prices** helps you work through unpriced items.

### Buy Again
Suggestions from previous purchases appear beneath the add controls when available. Tap a suggestion to open a prefilled item editor, review quantity and store, and save it. You can also use the main **Buy Again** action. Suggestions depend on available purchase history; do not assume a deleted item will remain in the suggestions forever.

### Finish the trip
Use **Clear Completed** for purchased items or **Clean Up Items** for broader deletion choices. Review the confirmation; deletion also affects the connected Apple Reminders list.

## 15 | Shopping with other people

A shopping list can belong to a shared Apple Reminders list. Sharing is managed through Apple Reminders and its account permissions, rather than by creating a separate TaskFlow shopping account.

### Prepare a shared list
Open the intended list in Apple Reminders and use its sharing controls to invite the other person. After the invitation is accepted, open TaskFlow and confirm the correct list is available. Each person needs access through the account that owns or shares the list.

### Shopper name
In **Settings > Shopping**, choose **Your Shopper Name** or **Add Shopper Name**. TaskFlow uses this name when recording supported add and purchase activity. It is a display name, not an account login or an invitation to share the list.

### Coordinate stores and quantities
Agree on consistent store names and units. Use per-item Store for exceptions. Before adding another copy of an item, check whether one already exists and whether increasing its quantity is the clearer choice.

### Duplicates
**Merge Duplicate Items** can help consolidate matching shopping entries. Review quantities and item differences first: similarly named products may have different units, stores, or purchase purposes.

### Be deliberate about deletion
Clearing completed or deleting items changes the actual Reminders list, so other participants can see those changes through syncing. Filtering the list only changes the view; deletion changes the data. TaskFlow's Clean Up screen includes items hidden by view filters, so inspect its count before confirming.

### When changes do not appear
Confirm that both people are viewing the same shared list. Check Reminders itself, account connectivity, and syncing before creating duplicate replacement lists. Some app settings are different from the shared reminder content.

**Tip:** use a recognizable shopper name and keep your store choices simple. The easier the list is to understand, the less coordination it requires.

## 16 | Watch Later: save and choose

Use **Reading & Watch Later** for saved movies, shows, videos, articles, and other media. A list named Watch Later can receive this layout through the setup suggestion, or you can choose it manually.

### Save a title or link
Use **Save Link**, enter or paste the link, and review the title. Choose an optional streaming service and add a note if useful. Save immediately; missing previews may be fetched afterward. From another app, use its Share menu to send supported content to TaskFlow.

### Streaming-service choices
Previously used service names are offered alongside the built-in choices. In **Edit Media**, select Streaming Service or enter another service. Link entries can also carry provider names, a country, and subscription or availability notes. A selected service is your saved context, not a live availability guarantee.

### A more visual list
Watching items appear under **Continue Watching**, ahead of **Saved for Later**. Completed media appears in **Finished**. Posters use a consistent small size and show a clean fallback when artwork is unavailable. A compact service badge keeps the row readable.

### Open and watch
Tap a title or poster for details. Use a saved watch link to open its destination. If multiple links are saved, choose the provider from the watch menu. Use **Episodes** directly on a tracked show's row to open episode tracking.

### Filters and artwork
Filter by media type or saved service. Active filters are shown with **Clear Filters**. If a preview fails, use **Retry Preview** or the artwork tools in details. Some shared links may not contain enough metadata to identify a specific release.

**Tip:** confirm the title, year, and format before combining similar entries. Different releases can share the same name.

## 17 | Match a show and track episodes

Episode tracking uses a matched show catalog. Saved links alone do not guarantee that a show is matched to the correct season and episode information.

### Match the show
1. Open **Match Show** from the list, or **Match Show & Track Episodes** from its detail screen.
2. Search for the title.
3. Compare the results and choose the correct show.
4. Check the show name and known seasons before recording progress.

Searching sends the title to TVmaze. Catalog information may include artwork, descriptions, cast, episode numbers, and release dates. Replacing an existing match resets that match's episode progress; read the confirmation first. Personal notes and saved links are retained by the replacement workflow.

### Your next episode
A tracked row shows **Next: S2 E4**, for example, and a small progress bar for the relevant season. The bar counts watched episodes against the known episodes in that season. Catalog information can be incomplete or change when refreshed.

### Watched Episode
Choose the prominent **Watched Episode** button to mark the current released episode watched and advance to the next eligible episode. The secondary **Episodes** button opens the full tracker; **Episode Progress** contains correction and catch-up tools.

### Season endings
After the last known episode in a season is marked through the quick action, choose whether to start the next known season, wait for more episodes, or mark the series finished. Finishing the last known episode does not automatically require you to finish the whole series.

### Unknown or future releases
Future or unknown release dates can limit which episodes are eligible for watched actions. A missing release date is not proof that a show has ended. Refresh Show Information when the catalog appears out of date.

## 18 | Correct progress, catch up, and avoid spoilers

Open **Episodes** on the main list row. The tracker places the relevant season first and expands it; other seasons are collapsed. Show descriptions are below the episode lists under **About the Show**.

### Correct your next episode
Open **Episode Progress > Change Season / Episode** and choose the episode you want next. The confirmation text explains that progress after the chosen point will be cleared. This is useful if you advanced too far or started tracking from the wrong position.

### Catch up in one step
Choose **Episode Progress > Watched Through** and select your last watched episode. Released episodes through that point are marked watched, instead of requiring you to tap each one. This action adds watched progress; changing the next episode is the option for moving backward.

### Undo an accidental action
Use **Undo Episode Change** while it is available in the progress menu, or the tracker's available Undo action. Undo refers to the most recent supported action; a later edit can replace it. Check the resulting next episode after undoing.

### Individual and season controls
Expand a season to mark or unmark individual episodes. **Mark Released Season Episodes Watched** handles the released portion of a season. Future episodes are not included simply because they are listed.

### Spoilers stay hidden
Episode rows show their number, runtime, and available release date. Episode names and descriptions are behind **Episode details**. Expand that control only when you want the details.

### A useful distinction
**Caught Up** means there is no currently eligible unwatched episode in the known schedule. **Finished** is the status you choose when you are done with the series. Refreshing a schedule can reveal new work without changing what you already watched.

## 19 | Upcoming releases and optional alerts

From Watch Later, open **Upcoming Releases** to review known scheduled episode releases. Treat these dates as catalog information rather than confirmation that your chosen streaming service will carry the episode in your country.

### Release reminders
Open the show's Episodes screen and enable **Release-day Notifications** when you want alerts. If notification access is needed, TaskFlow requests it. Leave the toggle off to track episodes without reminders.

When alerts are enabled, review the time of day, advance-reminder choice, and sound setting. Advance reminders depend on a known exact air time. When no exact time is available, the configured release-day time is used instead.

### One release day, multiple episodes
TaskFlow groups releases to avoid sending a separate alert for every episode in a full-season batch. Known-date alerts are scheduled from the saved show information. Unknown dates cannot generate a reliable release reminder.

### Notification actions
Where offered, a notification can let you mark an episode watched or snooze it. Read the episode identity before using the action. The app checks the matched show and eligibility; it does not treat a future episode as watched merely because a notification exists.

### Add a release to Calendar
Use the available add-to-calendar action from Upcoming Releases if you want a calendar entry. Review the event preview, calendar, and all-day or timed setting before saving. A calendar entry and a release notification are different choices.

### Keep schedules fresh
Use **Refresh Show Information** or the available refresh gesture. Background refresh and notification delivery depend on iOS, permissions, account connectivity, and current catalog information. Open TaskFlow periodically rather than relying on background updates alone.

**Tip:** use alerts selectively for shows you actually follow. A quiet Watch Later list can still track progress perfectly well.

## 20 | Projects, chores, packing, and routines

Specialized list layouts make different kinds of work easier to review without replacing the underlying reminders.

### Projects & Work
Use sections to group work, milestones to show progress toward a result, and Outcome to describe success. Record a Blocked Reason when progress depends on something else. Mark useful Next Actions so you can find actionable work across projects. A columns view is available where supported; tasks can be moved between project sections.

### Household & Chores
Group items by room and add instructions. Seasonal information helps distinguish occasional work from everyday chores. If you use repeat settings, check the next occurrence and any available last-completed information after finishing an item.

### Packing & Travel
Add the trip name and travel dates in list settings. Use quantities and categories, mark essentials, and choose among **Not Prepared, Prepared, and Packed**. **Still to Pack** narrows the list when preparing to leave. Starter or custom templates help avoid rebuilding the same packing list for each trip.

### Routines & Checklists
Use Step Order and sections to arrange the sequence. Instructions describe what to do; Timer Minutes can provide a timed step. **Complete & Next** moves the routine forward. A started timer can use a Lock Screen activity and an alert; review permissions when those features are not visible.

### Templates and repeated runs
Create or edit a template under **List Tools > Templates**. Review its items before starting. When a list supports separate runs, **Show Previous Runs** controls whether earlier work is visible. Hidden previous runs are not the same as deleted items.

**Tip:** use the simplest layout that makes the next action clear. A short routine often needs only titles and step order, while a complex project benefits from sections and blockers.

## 21 | Bills, errands, and appointments

These layouts are useful for work that needs context beyond a title.

### Bills & Renewals
Record Amount, Currency, Provider, Payment Link, and relevant renewal, notice, or cancellation deadlines. Use **Unpaid, Paid, or Canceled** to reflect the outcome. Keep a payment confirmation when helpful.

Open totals are grouped by currency; do not interpret them as an exchange-rate conversion. Review the due-date and deadline reminder settings. Deadline Reminders are available in list customization, and notifications still depend on permission and valid dates.

### Errands
Add Destination, Opening Hours, and Before Leaving information. A linked shopping list can connect an errand to its purchases. When using a location alert, select an actual location search result; text alone may not supply the coordinates needed for an arrival or departure trigger.

Check the destination and hours before travelling. A stored opening-hours note is not a live business-hours lookup.

### Appointments & Follow-ups
Use Contact and Preparation before the appointment, Questions during it, and Outcome afterward. Add a Follow-up Date for the next action. You can hide fields that are not useful through list customization.

### Tasks and events together
Use a calendar event to reserve the appointment time, and reminders for preparation or follow-up. Linking a note to the task or event helps keep supporting information nearby, but the two records do not automatically become the same item.

**Example:** create an appointment event, add a preparation reminder for the day before, and record the outcome in a linked note. Afterward, set a follow-up reminder instead of repeatedly editing the original appointment title.

## 22 | Search, tags, smart lists, and bulk changes

Use search and organization tools when your workspace grows beyond a few lists.

### Search and filters
Search from the main Search area or the search field in a list. Some task views let you choose a search scope and type **#tag**. Search text and filters can act together; clear both when a result seems unexpectedly absent.

### Tags
Add tags in task or note editors. Manage saved tags in Settings, where available rename and delete actions help keep the collection tidy. Tags can connect related work across separate lists without moving the items.

### Smart lists
Choose **New Smart List** and define rules for status, priority, due date, tag, or list. **All rules (AND)** requires each rule to match; **Any rule (OR)** includes items matching at least one rule. A smart list is a view of matching tasks, not a new place to store copies.

### Bulk actions
Use **Select Tasks** in the main task actions, or **Select Items** in supported specialized lists. Select the items you want and choose the available bulk action: completion, dates, moving, tagging, editing list fields, or deletion. Review the selection count before applying a change.

### Move versus view
Moving a task changes its owning list. Changing a filter, smart-list rule, sort order, or view mode changes what you see. If you only want a temporary view, use filters rather than moving or deleting records.

### Undo
Use the available Undo control soon after an accidental supported edit. A later action can replace the most recent Undo. For important bulk edits, inspect a few resulting items before continuing with other changes.

**Example:** create a smart list for high-priority tasks due soon, then keep the tasks in their original work and household lists.

## 23 | Widgets and the Lock Screen

TaskFlow widgets put selected information within reach without opening the full app. Available choices include task and agenda views, quick capture, smart-list views, shopping, media or episode views, and pinned notes.

### Add and configure
Use your device's Home Screen widget controls, choose TaskFlow, select a supported size, and add the widget. Touch and hold it and choose **Edit Widget** when that configuration is available. Review the chosen list or note rather than assuming every widget uses the same default.

### Shopping widgets
Choose a Shopping List, an optional Store, and **Maximum Items**. The selectable limit is up to **10**. An XL shopping widget is capped at 10 items; smaller widgets or larger accessibility text may display fewer items because of space. The limit controls displayed rows, not the number of purchases allowed in the list.

### Smart lists and media
Smart-list widgets can offer list selections, maximum items, and completed-item options. Media widgets can use a selected list or the automatic share-extension destination. Episode-related widgets use tracked shows and available release information.

### Pinned notes
Choose a note in a supported pinned-note widget. Checklist-related options can hide completed entries. Keep sensitive information in mind before putting a note on a screen others may see.

### Interactive actions
Use completion or episode actions when offered by the widget. Open TaskFlow to verify a change or troubleshoot an unavailable list. Widget refresh is controlled partly by the operating system and is not guaranteed to happen instantly after every remote edit.

### Live activities
TaskFlow offers settings for today's tasks on the Lock Screen, and routine timers can use Live Activities. Device support, system settings, and current activity state affect visibility.

## 24 | Clean up without losing track

Every reminder list has **Clean Up Items** in its list or More menu. Use it when you want an explicit selection and a count before deletion.

### Three deletion choices
- **Delete Completed:** remove completed items in that list.
- **Delete Selected:** choose individual items and remove the selection.
- **Delete All Items:** remove the list's items while retaining the list itself.

The cleanup screen includes items hidden by list filters. Selecting a parent includes its subtasks, and the confirmation count includes those included items. Read the count and message before confirming.

### What deletion affects
These actions delete actual Apple Reminders records and can sync to other devices and people sharing the list. Clearing a filter merely changes the view; deleting items changes the list data.

### Undo promptly
The cleanup screen offers the available Undo after a supported deletion. Use it before performing unrelated edits that can replace the latest Undo. Check the result after restoration, especially if you removed several items.

### Shopping cleanup
**Clear Completed** is also available for purchased shopping items. It works within its displayed shopping scope; the broader Clean Up screen is a different route that includes items hidden by view filters. Choose the route that matches your intent.

### Media cleanup
Marking a series Finished is not deletion. Keep finished media when you want a record of what you watched. Use cleanup only when you intend to remove the entries.

### Notes have separate controls
Web Notes offers Trash and Restore. Native notes have their own deletion, recovery, and history tools. Do not assume that reminder-list cleanup will manage notes, or that every delete action provides the same recovery path.

**Good practice:** select a few items first and inspect the count. Use Delete All only when you really want an empty list.

## 25 | Appearance, access, and everyday reliability

Settings collects permissions, appearance, task display, defaults, calendars, shopping identity, share destinations, notifications, tags, and sync controls.

### Make the app comfortable
Choose an appearance and theme when offered. Adjust Task Density, grouping, sorting, and Show Completed Tasks to suit the way you review work. Customize Today and specialized list fields independently rather than expecting one display setting to govern every screen.

### Larger text
The app adapts to accessibility text sizes by rearranging some controls. A widget or row may show fewer details or items to preserve readable text. A different arrangement is not necessarily missing functionality; open the full item or menu for additional controls.

### Accounts and sync
Tasks and events follow their Reminders and calendar accounts. TaskFlow notes and supported app information use TaskFlow's iCloud sync. **Sync Now** is useful for notes and app information, but it is not a universal reset button for every connected calendar provider.

### Offline expectations
Previously loaded information may remain visible when connectivity is unavailable. Check save or error indicators before assuming edits reached another device. Browser drafts can preserve unsaved text for recovery, but browser storage is not a substitute for a saved iCloud copy.

### Shared devices
Sign out of Web Notes on a shared computer. Choose carefully before displaying sensitive note content in a widget or sharing a task or attachment through system sharing controls.

### A simple maintenance routine
Occasionally clear obsolete filters, review default destinations, refresh show schedules, and remove unused tags or completed list entries. Keep a few important notes pinned rather than pinning everything. Update TaskFlow when a guide feature is absent from an older installed build.

## 26 | Troubleshooting and quick reference

### My list or task is missing
Check Reminders access, the owning account, and whether the item exists in Apple Reminders. Clear search and filters, inspect completed items, and check the selected list. A shared list may require an accepted sharing invitation.

### A calendar event is missing or cannot be edited
Check selected calendars and Calendar permission. Use Customize to include the intended calendar. Confirm that the calendar is writable before trying to edit or create an event there.

### An alert did not appear
Check system notification permission, TaskFlow's notification settings, the actual alert configuration, and whether the scheduled time is still in the future. For episodes, verify that alerts are enabled and a release date is known. System delivery can also depend on device settings.

### Web Notes will not open my notes
Use the same Apple Account as the native app. Open the current TaskFlow app, run Sync Now, wait for completion, then retry Continue with iCloud. If a save fails, preserve or download the draft before closing the browser. A sample-notes session is not your iCloud account.

### Shopping totals or items look wrong
Check the store filter, quantities, units, and missing-price count. Estimates exclude tax. Clear Filters to inspect the wider list. Default Store affects new items; it does not rewrite existing stores.

### Episode progress is wrong
Confirm the matched show, open Episodes, and use Change Season / Episode or Watched Through. Use Undo immediately after an accidental supported action. Refresh show information for missing catalog episodes; review unknown release dates before assuming the series is finished.

### Quick routes
- Daily priorities: **Today > Top 3 Today > Manage**.
- Shopping default: **List Tools > Customize List > Default Store**.
- Episode correction: **Episodes > Episode Progress**.
- Browser notes: **Notes menu > Open Notes in Browser**.
- Cleanup: **List Tools or More > Clean Up Items**.
- Permissions and syncing: **Settings**.

## 27 | Glossary and daily workflows

### Useful terms
**Task:** a reminder stored in a Reminders list. **Event:** a calendar entry with a time or all-day date. **List type:** TaskFlow's layout and fields for a reminder list. **Smart list:** a rule-based view of tasks. **Template:** a reusable set of list items. **Due date:** when work is expected. **Alert:** when you are notified. **Caught Up:** no currently eligible unwatched episode in the known schedule. **Finished:** media you have chosen to complete. **Draft:** work not yet confirmed as saved. **Recovered note:** a preserved version from a conflicting edit.

### A calm morning
Open Today, check your events, choose up to three priorities, and triage Focus Next. Use planning tools only for work that genuinely needs a time slot.

### A shopping trip
Open your grocery list, choose the store chip, review quantities, and purchase items with the large check-off control. Watch the estimate and missing prices. Finish with Buy Again or a deliberate cleanup of purchased entries.

### An evening of viewing
Open Continue Watching, confirm the next episode, and use the saved service link. After watching, tap Watched Episode. At a season ending, choose whether to continue, wait, or finish. Use Episodes for correction rather than creating a duplicate show.

### Working across phone and browser
Save your native note, run a sync when needed, and open Web Notes with the same Apple Account. Wait for a saved indication before switching devices. When a conflict appears, compare both versions and keep both if you are unsure.
